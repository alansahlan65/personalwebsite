Status: implemented as a 16:9 HyperFrames browser composition on 19 October 2026. The original 36-second direction below is retained as its source brief. The user's subsequent opening revision extends the current composition to 40 seconds. [Production notes](munchpal-opener.md) distinguish the review composition from the existing 20-second website video. MP4 replacement remains a separate delivery step.

## User-directed opening revision

After viewing the first composition, the user requested the whole logo and display text on frame one, followed by animated illustrated people interacting with the app, then the rest of the tour. This supersedes the row-first opening and row-only final-frame match below.

The current 40-second film opens on the original complete logo and “Food sharing, made clear.” Two illustrated people browse the source Food List from about 2.5 seconds. The central app screen crops to its selected food row and joins the original discovery beat at 7 seconds. All later tour beats move four seconds later, preserving their reading holds. The final frame matches the opening identity composition. The original food photographs remain unchanged; the people are new motion-study illustration assets.

## The idea

A meal appears within reach. A request makes the next step visible. A conversation brings the two people together. In a separate donation example, a report records the pickup.

The film follows the parts of MunchPal where someone decides what to do next. Each important control comes forward from its original screen, receives a deliberate tap, and opens the next state. Short claims punctuate that movement. The interface does the explaining.

Working title: **The next step is clear**. Closing line: **Food sharing, made clear**.

The feeling should be warm, assured, and responsive. MunchPal's food photography gives the film something human to look at; its orange conversation bubbles give the movement a recognizable shape. The central visual pleasure is watching an interface become legible at exactly the moment you need it.

## Purpose and audience

This sits at the top of the selected project and introduces the case study. Its audience is a prospective employer or collaborator assessing Alan's product design work. Within the first six seconds, they should recognize a food-sharing mobile product and understand one useful action. By the end, they should see how its request, conversation, and reporting states support different user needs.

Use the craft of a SaaS product tour: real components, deliberate interaction close-ups, claims followed by visible evidence. Keep MunchPal's actual mobile interface. No invented desktop dashboard, analytics, or B2B positioning.

The film is a later portfolio motion study of a prototype. The surrounding case-study caption retains that description. Designed states are not evidence of a deployed service, automatic acceptance, completed real pickups, or measured food-waste reduction.

## How the motion should feel

The camera should appear to understand what matters. It arrives just before the tap and follows the consequence. Use three deliberate interaction approaches: Request, Send message, and Donation Report. The confirmation action needs only a local reframe. Equal zooms on every button would make the movement predictable.

Use 0.65–0.9-second camera approaches with smooth acceleration and braking. At the target, allow 0.15–0.25 seconds of stillness before the tap. The control compresses subtly for 0.1 seconds and releases over about 0.2 seconds. The result starts after the press, never before it. These are motion-study timings, not measured app latency.

Lifted planes can move 24–48 pixels in screen space and turn no more than 3–5 degrees during travel. They return face-on before reading. Use a short, controlled overshoot on the first lift only. Chat bubbles and report fields should settle firmly without bouncing.

Text enters over 0.45–0.65 seconds, with a restrained directional blur that clears completely. Exits take 0.2–0.35 seconds. Keep display claims anchored to the screen, outside the camera rig. Alternate position according to the UI: left of the discovery row, centered during the orange pause, above the conversation, then alongside the report. Do not combine an eyebrow, title, and body paragraph.

The background spotlight continues moving during holds, but it is secondary. Every sustained beat also contains a meaningful UI action, a reveal, or a change of framing. No tiny camera breathing as the only activity.

## Composition and readability

Master at 1920×1080, 60 fps. Full phone screens establish context briefly. Lifted rows, buttons, bubbles, and report sections carry most of the film. Preserve each screen's proportions; do not stretch a portrait screen across a widescreen canvas.

Use display claims around 76–108 pixels, with a 64-pixel floor for the longer conditional line. Critical UI wording should appear at least 34 pixels high after scaling, and key conversational text around 40–44 pixels. If an original group cannot reach that size, crop closer. Incidental interface text may stay smaller when it is not required for understanding.

Keep crucial words and controls within a 96-pixel frame inset. A hero row can occupy roughly 55–65% of frame width; the phone does not need to be shown in full alongside it. Preserve source component spacing and corner geometry. Any later website spacing changes must follow DESIGN.md's shared 4/8px scale and tokens.

At thumbnail size, the opening must still show a food image, an actionable interface, and MunchPal identity. Check at the actual selected-project placement and at a 640-pixel-wide preview. If copy becomes unreadable, revise the crop or shorten the line before increasing animation speed.

## English flow and proposed UI edits

The display script progresses through discovery, action, contact, and documentation. Claims describe what the interface demonstrates. Avoid "instant," "effortless," "zero waste," "meals saved," and quantified impact.

The table below separates observed source text from suggested demo adaptations. Approval of this brief authorizes these wording choices for the later demo, not edits to the original research evidence or Figma file.

| Observed wording | Proposed demo treatment | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Older local list says "Ask"; live Figma component says "Request" | Refresh the export and use "Request" | Live Figma already resolves the ambiguity. Do not overlay new letters onto outlined old glyphs |
| "Are you sure you want to request this food?" / "Yes" | "Request this food?" / "Send request" | A shorter question and explicit action read faster in a close-up. This is a proposed adaptation |
| "Request sent!" | "Request sent" | Matches the quieter display punctuation; preserve the distinction from acceptance |
| "Your request has been accepted by Janu Suryajana. Please confirm!" | "Janu Suryajana accepted your request" with "Send message" | Removes an unclear instruction. Messaging is the visible next action |
| "Hi, is the food still available?" | Keep | Clear, natural question and authentic source sequence |
| "Hi Alan, of course. Please pick it up." | "Hi Alan, yes, it is. You can pick it up." | More conversational answer without adding a pickup time |
| "Thank you so much. I'll head over soon." / "Sure, I'll wait." | Keep | Natural exchange. It supports coordination, not proof of a completed pickup |
| "Garda Pangan has picked up your food donation. View the pickup report!" | "Garda Pangan has picked up your donation" with "View pickup report" | Shorter notification and clearer button. Retain Garda Pangan only inside this source example |
| "Donation Report," "Food Quantity," "Picked Up" | "Donation report," "Food quantity," "Picked up" | Consistent sentence case in any adapted labels |
| "20/11/2024" / "15.00 WIB" | "20 Nov 2024" / "15:00 WIB" | Easier English reading; retain the source date, time, and timezone |

Use MunchPal in editorial writing. Preserve the supplied wordmark artwork even where its lettering reads Munchpal. Do not redraw it to enforce capitalization.

## Sound and delivery assumptions

The core version is silent and must communicate without audio, appropriate for a portfolio opener. An optional sound version can use a restrained 100–110 BPM instrumental with dry taps, soft panel movement, and one fuller accent during the conversation reveal. Avoid notification sounds that imply real activity. No voiceover is proposed; the UI and claims carry the story. Music licensing and track selection belong to production.

After approval, build the reviewable browser composition with a deterministic timeline, hidden debug scrubber, and local assets. A standalone preview should open directly in a browser. Render MP4 only when requested for delivery. In the portfolio, retain the existing accessible pause/replay controls outside the artwork, reduced-motion stills, and offscreen pausing.

## What approval covers

Approve or revise the central idea, the nine-beat 36-second rundown, the orange-to-green visual progression, the conversation depth moment, and the proposed English adaptations. This document is the review artifact. Animation implementation and replacement of the current opener follow approval.

Before presenting the eventual animation, check:

- The request, confirmation, sent state, acceptance, and conversation are visibly distinct.
- The bulk report reads as another example rather than the outcome of the resident request.
- Each main tap has a clear target and an observable result.
- Original photographs, wordmark, and source values survive export and animation.
- The strongest depth effect occurs once; reading states remain front-facing.
- The opening communicates the product before a viewer scrolls away.
- Claims remain readable at the actual portfolio size without sound.
- No slide fades, three-feature grid, fabricated analytics, or unsupported outcomes appear.
- Key frames around 0, 3, 6, 9, 11, 14, 17, 20, 23, 26, 29, 32, and 35.5 seconds pass visual review.
- Forward play, reverse seeking, and the final-to-first loop show the same deterministic states.
